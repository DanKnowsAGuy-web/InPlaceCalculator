Methodology and pending confirmations

Asphalt thickness: AASHTO 1993 layered SN — a2 interpolated between 0.14 (CBR 80%) and 0.18 (CBR 100%) for conventional aggregate base, plus a3 = 0.11 for conventional sub-base where present; a2 = 0.20 for the In-Place treated base (the conservative bottom of the published stabilized-base range 0.20–0.27, Iowa SUDAS 5J-3 / FHWA-SA-98-042 — final value requires a project mix design). Remaining SN divided by a1 = 0.44.

Aggregate tonnage: layer volume × compacted density, default 140 lbs/CF (editable; typical dense-graded aggregate base 135–145 pcf). Asphalt tonnage: volume × 145 lbs/CF. Hauled volumes: bank volume × swell factor (default 1.25). Overhead and profit are % of direct cost; tax applies to the marked-up subtotal; the direct-cost subtotal is always shown separately.

Required SN: AASHTO 1993 flexible design equation solved from design ESALs, subgrade Mr (2554 × CBR^0.64, FHWA-HRT-12-030 (Powell et al.) / NCHRP 1-37A), reliability by road class (local 80% / collector 85% / arterial 90% / urban arterial 95%), S0 = 0.45, ΔPSI = 4.2 − 2.25. Minimum asphalt thickness enforced per the AASHTO minimum-thickness table (1.0″ at ≤50k ESALs up to 4.0″ above 7M); a surface treatment may replace asphalt only at ≤50k design ESALs, and chip seal carries zero structural credit.

Lifecycle: constant dollars discounted to NPV at the real discount rate (FHWA-SA-98-079 / OMB A-94); rehab schedule per alternative; salvage = remaining service life of the last rehab, prorated and discounted; chip-seal reseal cycle charged to the In-Place side when selected.

Deliberately excluded (with rationale): work-zone user delay costs — negligible on low-volume roads and their exclusion is conservative toward In-Place, which has fewer construction days (FHWA-SA-98-079 permits exclusion with justification); vehicle-operating-cost effects of surface condition — HDM-4 territory, not calibrated for this tool; drainage, right-of-way, and structures — equal under either alternative in a same-corridor comparison, so they cancel (Caltrans convention); mechanistic-empirical design (Pavement ME) — calibrated for ≥4″ asphalt sections, not thin/low-volume roads.

Pending confirmation: (1) In-Place treatment-pass labor — confirm with your operator's milling rate ÷ production. (2) Conventional duration defaults — field experience may tighten them. (3) In-Place a2 structural basis — pending 7-day UCS mix-design verification. (4) Product price — confirmed current (2026) manufacturer list price; negotiable for volume/contract pricing. (5) The base/subgrade maintenance share — planning placeholder (50% default); the often-quoted 78% figure is unsourced and permanently struck (NCHRP 602 is an unrelated climate/moisture model). (6) Wet/cold-climate durability — no high-rainfall or freeze-thaw laboratory series yet; the climate note above the sections states the current field record.

Like-for-like tiered layer credit (default) — model 3.6.2
Both sides now solve ONE shared required SN from native subgrade Mr (Mr = 2554 × CBR0.64) — standard AASHTO multi-layer treatment. The treated layer earns its own structural layer coefficient (a2, tiered by its CBR — see "Layer coefficients" above) rather than reducing the required SN itself. This replaces the model 3.6.1 "improved-subgrade Mr pathway," which modeled a thin treated layer sitting in the base-course position as if it were deep blended subgrade — denying it any layer role while the conventional side kept full base credit. Reviewed and rejected as the wrong physical model; like-for-like tiering uses identical correlations on both sides instead.
The 3.6.1 depth-weighted Mr-blend function (mrEffective) is retained in the code, unit-tested, but NOT used in this default calculation — kept for a possible future full-depth blended/reclamation mode where the treated material genuinely extends through the full stress-influence depth. Structural (a2) credit — UCS-unlock, beyond-base-class
The default already grants the treated layer like-for-like tiered credit (see "Layer coefficients" above) — the same CBR-based coefficient correlations the conventional aggregate base earns. A project-specific 7-day UCS test result unlocks credit BEYOND that base-class tier, into the published stabilized-base a2 classes (0.15/0.20/0.23 by SUDAS 5J-3 tier) — the claim that requires product-specific strength evidence. It is never defaulted, and it can only ADD credit on top of the CBR-based tier, never subtract from it. The only product-alone 7-day UCS result currently in the archive (GTS, North Dakota clay, 2011–12: 193–194 psi) falls below the 400 psi floor for any published stabilized-base tier.
Cement + product blends in the same GTS series reached 664–746 psi at 21 days (clearing the top 0.23 tier) — a distinct product configuration ("cement-blend mode") that is not offered by this tool and is noted here only as a flagged future opportunity (Wave 3 candidate), not a current default or unlock path.

Chip-seal gate — zero structural credit, natural form (model 3.6.2)
Chip seal is offered on the In-Place side, in BOTH locked and unlocked mode, when (1) design ESALs ≤ the surface-treatment threshold (default 50,000, per the AASHTO minimum-thickness table; editable for agency policy) AND (2) the treated base alone supplies the full required SN. The chip seal itself carries zero structural credit (Caltrans MTAG Ch. 7; WSDOT Report 841.1). A reseal cycle (default 6 yr) is charged to the In-Place side in the lifecycle.
Model 3.6.2 change: with the treated layer now carrying its own like-for-like tiered a2 by default, a strong granular soil (treated CBR ≥80, base-class tier) can legitimately reach chip-seal viability without a UCS test — the same AASHTO-published correlation that makes the conventional aggregate base viable at that CBR makes the treated layer viable too. The prior model 3.6.1 gate restricted chip seal to the UCS-unlocked state only, because the 3.6.1 default explicitly claimed no structural base layer at all.

How to read this in a procurement or engineering context

The estimator is strongest where it draws from AASHTO design math and FHWA LCCA practice — the structural-number solution, layer coefficients for conventional materials, minimum-thickness floors, and the NPV lifecycle method are grounded in standards engineers recognize, and can be presented with full citations.

It is more exposed where it relies on product field documentation — the treated-base a₂ value awaits a product-specific UCS series, the maintenance-share default is a labeled placeholder, and the climate record is arid-dominant. These are presented as planning-level projections, not engineering specifications.

Items tagged Pending should be resolved before using this estimator in a formal procurement, board presentation, or bid document. Every one is a user-editable field, and the printed estimate carries a full assumptions snapshot so reviewers can see exactly what was assumed.
